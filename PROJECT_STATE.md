## 1. Current Project Identity

Trapstar the Demo is a bounded, replayable city-block mystery game built in Unity.

The player investigates a stolen package over three in-game days while navigating Structured / Emergent / Negotiated systems: hard-tracked resources, social pressure, NPC motives, faction pressure, police heat, and player choice through the BASED interaction model.

The course-project “wow” is runtime gameplay AI: NPCs, factions, and police systems behaving dynamically inside a small, playable mystery scenario.

The game should demonstrate that a small environment can feel socially alive when hard systems, pressure systems, and negotiation choices constantly affect each other.

## 2. Core Pitch

A small block.  
Three days.  
Two factions.  
One stolen package.  
Several suspects.  
One or more guilty NPCs.  
One or more lying NPCs.  
One or more truth-telling NPCs.  
A police-monitored environment.  
A player trying to solve the case before time, pressure, reputation, or resources collapse.

The game is not an open-world city simulator. It is a tightly bounded social investigation prototype where Structured / Emergent / Negotiated gameplay creates replayable pressure.

The player moves through a world where:

- **Structured systems** track what is materially true.
- **Emergent systems** create social and environmental pressure.
- **Negotiated systems** let the player make choices through the BASED model.

## 6. Current Playable Prototype Target

Minimum playable loop:

1. The player enters the block.
2. A Mystery Director system assigns hidden case roles.
3. NPCs receive role, goal, faction, suspicion, and pressure states.
4. The player spends time moving, talking, observing, buying, selling, or investigating.
5. Structured systems update hard values such as time, money, items, BASED stats, player energy, health, and inventory.
6. Emergent systems respond through rumors, reputation, NPC goals, faction goals, personal motives, and police heat.
7. The player makes Negotiated choices through the BASED system.
8. NPCs respond based on hidden role, goal, faction loyalty, suspicion, pressure, police heat, and the player’s chosen BASED approach.
9. The player gathers clues.
10. Faction pressure and police heat change based on player behavior.
11. The player makes an accusation, negotiates a handoff, exposes the guilty party or parties, identifies deception, protects or flips witnesses, or fails before time runs out.

The first playable version should prove the loop, not the full dream game.

## 8. Structured / Emergent / Negotiated Direction

Structured / Emergent / Negotiated is the core design loop of Trapstar the Demo.

It should inform every part of the design at every level: world layout, NPC behavior, mystery logic, economy, time pressure, dialogue, player stats, police response, faction pressure, and course-facing AI presentation.

### Structured

Structured systems are anything hard-tracked by the game.

Examples:

- Time
- Money/Contra
- Items
- Inventory
- Clues
- Player location
- NPC location
- BASED stats
- Player energy
- Player health
- Faction values
- Police heat value
- Known/unknown mystery state
- Action costs
- Access permissions

Structured systems answer:

- What is true?
- What does the player have?
- What has changed numerically or materially?
- What can or cannot happen now?

### Emergent

Emergent systems are anything that creates pressure from the current situation.

Examples:

- Rumors
- Reputation
- Faction goals
- Personal NPC goals
- NPC suspicion
- NPC fear
- NPC loyalty
- Police heat
- Social consequences
- Public/private witness state
- Time pressure
- Scarcity
- Retaliation
- Opportunity

NPC goals are constrained Emergent pressure stats. They should not create unlimited behavior. They should explain and modify bounded behavior.

Example NPC goals:

- protect self
- protect faction
- protect another NPC
- hide guilt
- redirect blame
- sell information
- avoid police
- gain leverage
- recover package
- punish rival
- survive the three-day loop

Emergent systems answer:

- Who cares?
- Who reacts?
- Who is under pressure?
- What is getting worse or better because of the player’s actions?
- What new situation has emerged from the current state?

### Negotiated

Negotiated systems are player choices made through the BASED system.

BASED stands for:

- Belligerence
- Aggression
- Sociability
- Empathy
- Deception

Negotiated choices are not just dialogue flavor. They are gameplay moves that spend time, risk consequences, and change the state of the mystery, NPCs, factions, police heat, reputation, and access.

Negotiated systems answer:

- How does the player approach the situation?
- What tone, tactic, or social move does the player use?
- What does the player risk?
- What does the player gain?
- How does the world respond?

The core loop:

1. Structured state defines the situation.
2. Emergent pressure makes the situation unstable.
3. The player Negotiates through BASED choices.
4. The result changes the Structured state.
5. New Emergent pressure appears.
6. The loop continues.

## 9. Stolen Package Mystery Direction

The Stolen Package case is the main scenario.

Current known structure:

- One or more NPCs may be guilty.
- One or more NPCs may be lying.
- One or more NPCs may be telling the truth.
- Each NPC may act from a different constrained goal.
- Goals are Emergent pressure stats that shape how NPCs respond.
- Two factions are involved.
- The player has three in-game days.
- Each run can randomize role assignment, goal assignment, and faction conditions within bounded limits.

The mystery must always remain solvable.

The Stolen Package case should be designed through the Structured / Emergent / Negotiated loop.

Structured mystery elements:

- Package exists.
- Package has an origin.
- Package has a current hidden state or location.
- NPCs have assigned roles.
- NPCs have constrained goal states.
- Clues exist in fixed categories.
- Time passes.
- The player collects evidence.
- Resolution conditions are tracked.

Emergent mystery elements:

- NPCs protect themselves.
- NPCs pursue constrained personal goals.
- Factions pressure witnesses.
- Rumors spread.
- Police heat increases.
- Guilty NPCs may redirect blame.
- Lying NPCs may conceal or distort useful truth.
- Truth-telling NPCs may become harder to access.
- Player reputation affects cooperation.

Negotiated mystery elements:

- The player questions NPCs through BASED choices.
- The player trades, pressures, lies, empathizes, or threatens.
- The player chooses when to reveal clues.
- The player decides who to accuse.
- The player can negotiate handoffs, protection, silence, exposure, or cooperation.

The system should avoid fully unbounded procedural mystery generation. It should use controlled variation:

- randomized suspect role assignment
- randomized NPC goal assignment
- randomized faction pressure
- randomized clue access order
- randomized NPC cooperation/suspicion state
- fixed clue categories
- fixed resolution conditions

## 10. Faction and Police Direction

Factions create social pressure.

Faction systems may track Structured values such as:

- faction strength
- faction resources
- faction suspicion
- faction hostility
- faction protection
- faction relationship to each NPC
- faction relationship to the player
- faction control over locations

Faction systems may create Emergent pressure through:

- threats
- rumors
- protection
- retaliation
- restricted access
- witness intimidation
- shifting loyalties
- offers or demands

The player Negotiates with or against factions through BASED choices:

- Belligerence may provoke or intimidate.
- Aggression may force short-term results but raise heat.
- Sociability may build access.
- Empathy may lower suspicion or unlock trust.
- Deception may bypass obstacles but create later risk.

Police create external pressure.

Police systems may track Structured values such as:

- police heat
- public disturbance
- known suspicious actions
- player record/reputation
- patrol state
- day/time escalation
- investigation deadline

Police systems may create Emergent pressure through:

- patrol presence
- questioning
- restricted spaces
- witness fear
- faction caution
- sudden interruptions
- forced movement
- risk of arrest or failure

The player Negotiates around police pressure through BASED choices, movement choices, timing choices, and public/private behavior.

Faction and police systems should not overwhelm the prototype. Their purpose is to make the Structured / Emergent / Negotiated mystery more dynamic.

## 11. Map / Traversal Direction

The map should be small, readable, and implementation-friendly.

Current direction:

- One city block or looping-room-style environment
- Side-alley exit nodes
- Several enterable buildings
- NPC-populated social spaces
- Police-monitored public areas
- Faction-influenced zones
- Movement should cost time or create exposure

The map should be designed through the Structured / Emergent / Negotiated loop.

Structured map elements:

- locations
- entrances/exits
- locked/unlocked access
- NPC positions
- item locations
- clue locations
- time cost of movement
- public/private space flags
- faction-controlled zones
- police-monitored zones

Emergent map pressure:

- who sees the player
- where rumors can spread
- where police heat matters most
- where faction retaliation is likely
- where NPCs feel safe or threatened
- which paths become risky based on time/day
- which locations become more or less useful as the mystery changes

Negotiated map choices:

- where the player chooses to go
- who the player chooses to approach
- whether to act publicly or privately
- whether to spend time moving, hiding, talking, trading, or investigating
- whether to enter faction space, police-visible space, or neutral space

Traversal should support investigation, pressure, and repeated interaction, not large-scale exploration.

## 12. Time / Contra / Economy Direction

Time is a core Structured pressure system.

Current direction:

- Player has three in-game days.
- Actions cost minutes.
- Movement, dialogue, investigation, buying, selling, and recovery may consume time.
- Time pressure should force tradeoffs.
- Contra/money supports legal and illicit goods/services.
- Personal health/energy systems constrain how much the player can do.

The Time / Contra / Economy system should be designed through the Structured / Emergent / Negotiated loop.

Structured economy elements:

- minutes remaining
- day number
- money/Contra
- inventory
- goods/services
- item prices
- action costs
- health
- energy
- access requirements

Emergent economy pressure:

- scarcity
- rising prices
- faction discounts or markups
- police risk
- debt
- favors owed
- rumors from purchases/sales
- NPC needs
- temptation to take shortcuts

Negotiated economy choices:

- buy
- sell
- trade
- threaten
- bargain
- lie
- call in a favor
- pay for information
- refuse a deal
- spend time to recover
- risk health/energy to save time

Economy systems should remain light in the first playable version.

## 17. Current Design Principle

Trapstar the Demo should be:

- bounded, not sprawling
- replayable, not infinite
- readable, not opaque
- systemic, not overcomplicated
- agentic, not random
- playable before polished
- course-ready before dream-complete

The player should feel that the block is socially alive, but the developer should always be able to explain why something happened.

Every system should obey the core loop:

1. **Structured:** What is being tracked?
2. **Emergent:** What pressure does that create?
3. **Negotiated:** What BASED choice can the player make?
4. **Consequence:** What changes afterward?

No system should be added unless it strengthens that loop.
