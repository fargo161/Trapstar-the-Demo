## 1. Current Project Identity

Trapstar the Demo is a bounded, replayable city-block mystery game built in Unity.

The player investigates the **Stolen Package** case over three in-game days while navigating Structured / Emergent / Negotiated systems: hard-tracked resources, social pressure, NPC motives, faction pressure, police heat, and player choice through the Deal / Pressure / Ask frame, colored by BASED traits and Vibes.

The course-project “wow” is runtime gameplay AI: NPCs, factions, and police systems behaving dynamically inside a small, playable mystery scenario.

The game should demonstrate that a small environment can feel socially alive when hard systems, pressure systems, and negotiation choices constantly affect each other.

## 2. Core Pitch

A small block.  
Three days.  
Two factions.  
One stolen package.  
Several suspects.  
One or more guilty NPCs.  
One or more lying NPCs.  
One or more truth-telling NPCs.  
A police-monitored environment.  
A player trying to solve the case before time, pressure, reputation, or resources collapse.

The game is not an open-world city simulator. It is a tightly bounded social investigation prototype where Structured / Emergent / Negotiated gameplay creates replayable pressure.

The player moves through a world where:

- **Structured systems** track what is materially true.
- **Emergent systems** create social and environmental pressure.
- **Negotiated systems** let the player choose a Deal / Pressure / Ask frame, colored by BASED traits and Vibes.

## 6. Current Playable Prototype Target

Minimum playable loop:

1. The player enters the block.
2. A Mystery Director system assigns hidden case roles.
3. NPCs receive role, goal, faction, suspicion, and pressure states.
4. The player spends time moving, talking, observing, buying, selling, or investigating.
5. Structured systems update hard values such as time, money, items, BASED stats, player energy, health, and inventory.
6. Emergent systems respond through rumors, reputation, NPC goals, faction goals, personal motives, and police heat.
7. The player chooses one DPA frame for the situation: Deal, Pressure, or Ask.
8. The player's DPA frame is colored by a BASED trait, Vibe, or approach.
9. NPCs respond based on hidden role, goal, faction loyalty, suspicion, pressure, police heat, available info/assets, and the player’s chosen DPA/BASED approach.
10. The player gathers clues.
11. Faction pressure and police heat change based on player behavior.
12. The player makes an accusation, negotiates a handoff, exposes the guilty party or parties, identifies deception, protects or flips witnesses, or fails before time runs out.

The first playable version should prove the loop, not the full dream game.

## 8. Structured / Emergent / Negotiated Direction

Structured / Emergent / Negotiated is the core design loop of Trapstar the Demo.

### Structured

Structured systems are anything hard-tracked by the game: Time, Money/Contra, items, inventory, clues, locations, BASED stats, player energy/health, faction values, police heat, known/unknown mystery state, action costs, and access permissions.

Structured systems answer:

- What is true?
- What does the player have?
- What has changed numerically or materially?
- What can or cannot happen now?

### Emergent

Emergent systems are anything that creates pressure from the current situation: rumors, reputation, faction goals, NPC goals, NPC suspicion/fear/loyalty, police heat, witnesses, scarcity, retaliation, and opportunity.

NPC goals are constrained Emergent pressure stats. They should not create unlimited behavior. They should explain and modify bounded behavior.

Emergent systems answer:

- Who cares?
- Who reacts?
- Who is under pressure?
- What is getting worse or better because of the player’s actions?
- What new situation has emerged from the current state?

### Negotiated

Negotiated systems are player choices made through the Deal / Pressure / Ask frame, colored by BASED traits and Vibes.

DPA is not an ordered loop. It is the player-facing strategic choice frame used at meaningful decision points.

DPA maps symbolically to SEN:

```text
Deal     = Logos / Structured / hard reality
Pressure = Pathos / Emergent / dynamic force
Ask      = Ethos / Negotiated / social choice
```

BASED stands for Belligerence, Aggression, Sociability, Empathy, and Deception.

Negotiated systems answer:

- Which DPA frame does the player choose?
- What BASED trait, Vibe, tone, tactic, or social move colors the approach?
- What does the player risk?
- What does the player gain?
- How does the world respond?

The core loop:

1. Structured state defines the situation.
2. Emergent pressure makes the situation unstable.
3. The player chooses a DPA frame, colored by a BASED approach.
4. The result changes the Structured state.
5. New Emergent pressure appears.
6. The loop continues.

## 9. Stolen Package Mystery Direction

The **Stolen Package** case is the main scenario. The package may be represented in-world as a missing Contra shipment/package.

The mystery must always remain solvable and bounded.

Current known structure:

- one or more NPCs may be guilty
- one or more NPCs may be lying
- one or more NPCs may be telling the truth
- each NPC may act from a different constrained goal
- two factions are involved
- the player has three in-game days
- each run can randomize role assignment, goal assignment, and faction conditions within bounded limits

Negotiated mystery elements:

- The player questions NPCs through Deal, Pressure, or Ask.
- The player's DPA frame is colored by BASED traits or Vibes.
- The player trades, pressures, lies, empathizes, threatens, bargains, or appeals.
- The player chooses when to reveal clues.
- The player decides who to accuse.
- The player can negotiate handoffs, protection, silence, exposure, or cooperation.

## 10. Faction and Police Direction

Factions create social pressure. Police create external pressure.

Faction systems may track strength, resources, suspicion, hostility, protection, relationships, and control over locations.

Police systems may track police heat, public disturbance, known suspicious actions, player record/reputation, patrol state, day/time escalation, and investigation deadline.

The player negotiates with factions and around police pressure through DPA choices, BASED approach, movement choices, timing choices, and public/private behavior.

Faction and police systems should not overwhelm the prototype. Their purpose is to make the Structured / Emergent / Negotiated mystery more dynamic.

## 12. Time / Contra / Economy Direction

Time is a core Structured pressure system.

Current direction:

- Player has three in-game days.
- Actions cost minutes.
- Movement, dialogue, investigation, buying, selling, and recovery may consume time.
- Time pressure should force tradeoffs.
- Contra/money supports legal and illicit goods/services.
- Personal health/energy systems constrain how much the player can do.

Economy systems should remain light in the first playable version.

## 14. Current Risk List

High risk:

- Scope creep from open-world ambitions.
- Too many systems before the core mystery loop works.
- Runtime AI becoming invisible or hard to evaluate.
- Dialogue complexity expanding too quickly.
- Structured / Emergent / Negotiated becoming terminology instead of playable logic.
- Deal / Pressure / Ask becoming an ordered loop instead of a player-facing choice frame.
- Multi-NPC guilt/lie/truth states becoming too complex before the basic mystery loop works.

Medium risk:

- Unity implementation complexity.
- Codex producing code before the design contracts are clear.
- Faction/police systems becoming bigger than the mystery.
- Visual polish distracting from playable logic.
- BASED choices becoming cosmetic instead of mechanically meaningful.
- NPC goals becoming too numerous or too vague.

## 15. Current Design Principle

Trapstar the Demo should be:

- bounded, not sprawling
- replayable, not infinite
- readable, not opaque
- systemic, not overcomplicated
- agentic, not random
- playable before polished
- course-ready before dream-complete

Every system should obey the core loop:

1. **Structured:** What is being tracked?
2. **Emergent:** What pressure does that create?
3. **Negotiated:** What DPA frame and BASED approach can the player choose?
4. **Consequence:** What changes afterward?

No system should be added unless it strengthens that loop.
